What survives when June is left out?

A fixed-vintage check of 19,871 residential sale records in eight university postal towns. The sample's geographical shares move little, but its two largest towns swap places.

Matt Lenzie · 2 November 2026 · Supporting analysis of SAF-H1-2026

Scope first. These are general residential sales, not identified student HMOs or PBSA transactions. Removing June tests sensitivity to one month. It does not repair registration lag or reveal final market activity.

The question this check answers

The H1 2026 Student Accommodation Finance Report includes an eight-town residential context series from a retained Land Registry file dated 6 August 2026. June has less time to acquire registrations than earlier months. A reader using the sample to decide which towns dominate the evidence should ask: does its geographical composition depend heavily on that final month?

This note drops June from the published monthly counts and recalculates each town's share of the remaining records. It uses the same frozen public evidence package, compiled on 12 September, rather than a newer Land Registry download. It adds a reproducible sensitivity calculation to the existing study; it adds no newly observed transactions.

June contributes 6.81% of this observed H1 sample. After removing it, the largest absolute change in any town's sample share is 0.263 percentage points, for Sheffield. This describes stability in the distribution of recorded counts across the selected towns, not completeness of the observations.

The leading two towns reverse order. Bristol has 4,064 H1 records against Manchester's 4,053, a lead of 11. Without June, Manchester has 3,795 against Bristol's 3,767, a lead of 28. A small difference in sample size therefore supports a fragile ranking even when the percentage shares look stable.

The other six positions remain unchanged. June's share within each town ranges from 4.74% in Sheffield to 8.06% in Leeds. That variation is observable; these aggregates cannot separate registration timing, seasonality, genuine activity differences or changes in property mix.

Calculation you can reproduce

For town i, let Hi be its H1 count and Ji its June count:

Retained count = H_i - J_i
H1 sample share = 100 * H_i / 19,871
Jan-May sample share = 100 * (H_i - J_i) / 18,517
Share change (percentage points) = Jan-May share - H1 share

The Python script checks all six monthly labels, reconciles each town's monthly counts with its H1 total and confirms that the changes in sample share sum to zero before rounding. It uses only the Python standard library. Run python3 analyse.py in the folder containing the source JSON to regenerate the CSV and results file.

Where the conclusion stops

January to May is not a fully matured comparison period simply because June has been removed. Older transactions can also register later. This exercise cannot estimate missing records, prove a fall in liquidity, justify an uplift, or establish a seasonal pattern. It is a check on the composition of one fixed file.

The published H1 town medians must not be carried across as January-to-May medians. Monthly counts contain no transaction prices, so they cannot reproduce those medians or a pooled eight-town median. No price, rental yield, value per bed, lender market share or finance availability is estimated here.

A stronger follow-up would repeat the same filters against a later source vintage and compare additions by transaction month and town. That would measure backfill directly. No later-vintage comparison has been performed for this note.

Source, provenance and rights

The source population comprises H1-dated detached, semi-detached, terraced and flat transactions in eight exact postal-town fields. Deleted records are excluded and unique transaction GUIDs are counted. Postal towns are not local-authority boundaries or university catchments. See the report's full selection and registration-lag methodology and versioned public evidence repository.
